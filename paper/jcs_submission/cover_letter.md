Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript, “Do physics-informed losses add value beyond smoothness? Controlled comparisons for neural PDE surrogates,” for publication as a Research Article in the *Journal of Computational Science*.

The manuscript studies a computationally important question in PDE surrogate modeling: whether prediction gains from a physics residual persist when compared with matched gradient and curvature regularizers under a fixed neural operator and training protocol. Its primary evidence is a registered comparison on a locked 500-trajectory PDEBench Burgers holdout. We also report sparse-time, noisy-label, resolution, and sample-count outcomes, including negative results, and a same-architecture pointwise-versus-interval comparison on a synthetic periodic Allen-Cahn development pool. The latter is explicitly identified as reused development evidence, not independent confirmation.

We do not present a new network or residual formulation. The manuscript's contribution is a controlled, provenance-aware comparison that distinguishes improvement over data-only fitting from evidence of a unique physics-specific gain, while retaining field, derivative, and data-use limitations in the main text. We believe this focus on computational modeling and simulation for PDE surrogates is relevant to the journal's scope.
